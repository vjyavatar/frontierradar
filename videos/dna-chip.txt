FrontierRadar explainer: A porous silica chip writes 11× more DNA per spot, a step to cheaper 'DNA drives'
Issue: 2026-10-30 | Stage 1 · Lab proof | Readiness 34/100 | Voice: Piper en_US-lessac-medium (local)

HOOK
A chip surface just made 11 times more DNA on the same spot.

WHAT IT IS
It is a porous silica film that boosts how much DNA each write site on a chip can produce.

ANALOGY
Think of a flat car park versus a multi-storey one on the same plot. The footprint is identical, but the levels hold far more cars.

HOW IT WORKS
First, electrodes on the chip build DNA letter by letter, using chip-making-friendly chemistry. Second, the porous film gives each site far more surface to grow on.

BEFORE
Before, each write site made only a little DNA, and writing is the costliest step in DNA storage.

AFTER
Now yield passes 1,821 picomoles per square centimetre, over 11 times a flat surface, with error rates comparable to other lab systems.

WHY IT MATTERS
Cheaper writing is the road to DNA drives. Twist Bioscience and chip foundries could gain. Lab stage. Our judgment: watch. Not financial advice.
