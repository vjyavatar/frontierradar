HOOK
A magnetic-electric material just kept working at 1.6 nanometres.

WHAT IT IS
Brown University made thin bismuth ferrite films with no dead layer.

ANALOGY
Think of a stadium card display. Fans flip cards when whispered to, but the edge rows were always asleep. Now every row joins in.

HOW IT WORKS
First, the team removed the dead layer. Second, electric and magnetic order survived down to four atomic unit cells.

BEFORE
Before, these films lost their properties near the surface.

AFTER
Now they work at about 1.6 nanometres, with signs of altermagnetism.

WHY IT MATTERS
Voltage-flipped memory could slash energy. Fundamental physics, manufacturing far off. Our judgment: wait. Not financial advice.
