FrontierRadar explainer: Brain-like chip that can learn on the device (neuromorphic)
Issue: 2026-10-30 | Stage 1 · Paper | Readiness 28/100 | Voice: Piper en_US-lessac-medium (local)

HOOK
A brain-like chip learns on the spot, and beat Nvidia's Jetson by up to 3 times.

WHAT IT IS
It is a neuromorphic chip design that trains itself on the device.

ANALOGY
Think of a chess player who improves during the game, learning from each opponent's move at the board, instead of only studying at a far-away training camp.

HOW IT WORKS
First, the chip works with brain-style spikes. Second, it adjusts its own settings on the device, without sending data to a data centre.

BEFORE
Before, learning happened in data centres.

AFTER
Now on an FPGA it beat Jetson Orin 1.9 to 3.3 times, with less memory traffic than an A100.

WHY IT MATTERS
On-device learning for edge AI is a real gap, but this is an FPGA demo, not a product. BrainChip, Lattice and AMD are the exposure. Our judgment: watch. Not financial advice.
