HOOK
A new solid battery electrolyte kept 81 percent capacity after 2,000 fast charges.

WHAT IT IS
It is an amorphous zirconium oxyhalide electrolyte for solid-state batteries.

ANALOGY
Think of a harbour wall. High tides crack ordinary walls, but this wall grows its own breakwater where the waves hit hardest.

HOW IT WORKS
First, lithium ions move fast through it, 4.11 millisiemens per centimetre at room temperature. Second, a protective layer forms at the cathode.

BEFORE
Before, many fast solid electrolytes broke down above about 4.3 volts.

AFTER
Now cells kept up to 81.2 percent capacity after 2,000 cycles at 10C.

WHY IT MATTERS
Higher voltage means higher-energy cells. Lab cells only. Solid-state developers like QuantumScape are the exposure. Our judgment: wait. Not financial advice.
