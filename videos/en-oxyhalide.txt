FrontierRadar explainer: A new solid electrolyte keeps 81% capacity after 2,000 fast charges at high voltage
Issue: 2026-10-30 | Stage 1 · Lab cell | Readiness 32/100 | Voice: Piper en_US-lessac-medium (local)

HOOK
Batteries without liquid could charge faster and be safer.

WHAT IT IS
A new solid electrolyte keeps 81% capacity after 2,000 fast charges at high voltage.

WHAT IT DOES
An amorphous zirconium oxyhalide electrolyte reaches 4.11 mS/cm at 25 °C and forms a self-protecting layer at the cathode. Think of it like this: Like a road that paves over its own potholes as heavy trucks drive on it.

BEFORE
Many fast-conducting solid electrolytes break down above about 4.3 V, blocking high-energy cathodes.

AFTER
Who could benefit: Solid-state battery developers, e.g. QuantumScape QS, Solid Power SLDP.

WHY IT MATTERS
We rate it Stage 1, Lab cell, readiness 32 out of 100. The catch: Lab coin-type cells with Li-In alloy anode. Our judgment: wait. Not financial advice.
