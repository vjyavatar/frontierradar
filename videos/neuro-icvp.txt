FrontierRadar explainer: A wireless brain implant for artificial vision goes into a third blind patient
Issue: 2026-10-31 | Stage 2 · Pilot / feasibility | Readiness 33/100 | Voice: Piper en_US-lessac-medium (local)

HOOK
A blind patient just received a wireless implant that talks straight to the brain's vision area.

WHAT IT IS
It is the Intracortical Visual Prosthesis, now placed in a third person at Rush University Medical Center.

ANALOGY
Think of a stadium light board. Each bulb is one dot, and a pattern of dots draws a rough picture, even when the TV camera is broken.

HOW IT WORKS
First, 34 tiny wireless stimulators with 544 electrodes sit in the visual cortex. Second, they send patterns that may be seen as spots of light.

BEFORE
Before, most sight aids needed a working eye or optic nerve.

AFTER
Now three people have the implant, and the study will test whether it helps them move around.

WHY IT MATTERS
No results on what participants see are published yet. Partners are mostly private. Our judgment: wait. Not financial advice.
