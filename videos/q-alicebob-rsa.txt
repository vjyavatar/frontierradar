FrontierRadar explainer: Alice & Bob: about 22,600 'cat qubits' could break RSA-2048 encryption in about 3 days, on paper
Issue: 2026-10-31 | Stage 1 · Lab physics | Readiness 22/100 | Voice: Piper en_US-lessac-medium (local)

HOOK
The quantum computer needed to break bank encryption just got 40 times smaller, on paper.

WHAT IT IS
Alice and Bob, a French quantum startup, published a full plan for running Shor's code-breaking algorithm on its cat qubits.

ANALOGY
Think of an engineer who publishes complete blueprints showing a bridge needs 40 times fewer steel beams than anyone assumed. Nothing is built yet, but the price tag just shrank.

HOW IT WORKS
First, cat qubits block one kind of error in hardware, so less correction is needed. Second, the team laid out every step, down to the physical routing on the chip.

BEFORE
Before, estimates for standard qubits ran to hundreds of thousands or millions of qubits.

AFTER
Now the estimate is about 22,600 cat qubits and roughly 3 days to break RSA-2048.

WHY IT MATTERS
It is a paper estimate that assumes hardware nobody has built yet. But it pulls the deadline for upgrading encryption closer. Our judgment: watch. Not financial advice.
