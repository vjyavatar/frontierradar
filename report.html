2. DuoPD: small compute units inside HBM4 memory could make AI chatbots answer up to 2.2× faster

🏛 Seoul National University (Jae-Joon Kim group) · 📅 2026-10-29

What they found: The authors add lightweight processing-near-memory units to the logic die at the bottom of HBM4 stacks. The GPU handles the prompt and the memory handles word-by-word answering, sharing the same HBM. In simulation this gives up to 2.23× more throughput and up to 37.9% energy saving, for 6.51 mm² and 6.45 W per stack.

🧒 Like I'm 10: The computer's brain keeps running to its notebook. This idea puts a little helper inside the notebook so it can do some of the work itself.

🧩 Think of it like: Instead of running to the library for every sentence, the librarian writes the answer at the shelf and only hands you the result.

3. Brown University: a magnetic-electric material that keeps working at 1.6 nm, a step toward ultra-low-power memory

🏛 Brown University and collaborators (Nature Materials) · 📅 2026-10-28

What they found: Thin films of bismuth ferrite (BiFeO3) usually lose their properties near the surface, called a "dead layer". The team removed it, keeping electric and magnetic order down to four atomic unit cells (about 1.6 nm), and found signs of altermagnetism.

🧒 Like I'm 10: Scientists found a way to make a special material work even when it is thinner than a strand of DNA. One day it could make memory chips use much less power.

🧩 Think of it like: Like a paint that used to stop working if you brushed it on too thin; now even a near-invisible coat still works.
