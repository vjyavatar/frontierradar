FrontierRadar explainer: PsiQuantum shows a light-based switch that rewires AI data-centre networks in 3 ms
Issue: 2026-10-30 | Stage 2 · Prototype | Readiness 46/100 | Voice: Piper en_US-lessac-medium (local)

HOOK
What if AI data centres could stop translating light into electricity?

WHAT IT IS
PsiQuantum, a quantum-computing company, has built a light-based switch for AI data-centre networks. The chips are made at GlobalFoundries.

WHAT IT DOES
It points beams of light from one computer to another, and can rewire the network in under 3 milliseconds. Think of a railway junction that swaps tracks in a blink, so data trains of light never have to stop.

BEFORE
Until now, most network switches turn light into electricity and back again, which costs power and adds delay. Google already uses switches built from tiny moving mirrors.

AFTER
PsiQuantum offers a chip-sized version from a standard factory, with 1.2 dB of signal loss. Who could benefit: PsiQuantum, GlobalFoundries, and big cloud firms building AI clusters.

WHY IT MATTERS
We rate it Stage 2, Prototype, with a readiness score of 46 out of 100. Samples are going to select customers, but results are company-reported, and only 8 by 8 ports so far. Our judgment: watch for a named customer and a bigger switch. Not financial advice.
