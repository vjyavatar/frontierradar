FrontierRadar explainer: AtlasBase unveils 'Thalia', a DNA-writing chip with 5.6 billion sites, 700× earlier chips
Issue: 2026-10-31 | Stage 2 · Prototype system | Readiness 36/100 | Voice: Piper en_US-lessac-medium (local)

HOOK
A new chip can write DNA at 5.6 billion spots at once.

WHAT IT IS
AtlasBase unveiled Thalia, a semiconductor chip that writes synthetic DNA for data storage and DNA computing.

ANALOGY
Think of going from one printing press to a newspaper plant with billions of tiny presses printing side by side.

HOW IT WORKS
First, each electrode site builds a DNA strand letter by letter. Second, billions of sites work in parallel on a chip made with standard chip lines.

BEFORE
Before, writing DNA was the slowest and costliest step of DNA storage.

AFTER
Now AtlasBase claims a 700-fold scale-up over earlier chips, with early access for partners.

WHY IT MATTERS
No speed or cost per gigabyte was disclosed, so this is early. Our judgment: wait. Not financial advice.
