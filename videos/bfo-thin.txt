FrontierRadar explainer: Brown University: a magnetic-electric material that keeps working at 1.6 nm, a step toward ultra-low-power memory
Issue: 2026-10-30 | Stage 1 · Paper | Readiness 16/100 | Voice: Piper en_US-lessac-medium (local)

HOOK
Can a material thinner than a strand of DNA still keep its magic?

WHAT IT IS
Brown University and collaborators, in Nature Materials, studied thin films of bismuth ferrite, a material that is both magnetic and electric.

WHAT IT DOES
They removed the dead layer near the surface, so the material keeps working down to about 1.6 nanometres. Like paint that used to fail if brushed too thin; now even a near-invisible coat still works.

BEFORE
Until now, very thin films of this material lost their properties near the surface. That blocked the tiny sizes real devices need.

AFTER
Materials like this could one day let voltage, not current, flip memory bits, cutting energy use. For now, memory and spintronics research labs benefit.

WHY IT MATTERS
We rate it Stage 1, Paper, with a readiness score of 16 out of 100. The films grow on a rare crystal, not standard silicon. Our judgment: wait. Watch for growth on silicon and a working memory cell. Not financial advice.
