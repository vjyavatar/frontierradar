FrontierRadar explainer: A light switch sending 200 billion bits per second per colour
Issue: 2026-10-30 | Stage 1 · Paper | Readiness 36/100 | Voice: Piper en_US-lessac-medium (local)

HOOK
A tiny chip switch now flips light 200 billion times a second.

WHAT IT IS
It is a silicon slow-light modulator for data-centre optics.

ANALOGY
Think of a camera shutter clicking open and shut so fast that each click is one bit of data.

HOW IT WORKS
First, slowing light boosts its interaction with silicon. Second, the device switches it on and off at high speed.

BEFORE
Before, per-lane speed limited optics.

AFTER
Now 200 gigabits per second fit on one colour.

WHY IT MATTERS
Lab result, but on transceiver roadmaps. Marvell, Broadcom and Coherent are exposure. Our judgment: watch. Not financial advice.
