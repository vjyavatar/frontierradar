FrontierRadar explainer: A bedside brain-wave cap let 1 in 3 'unresponsive' patients control a sound
Issue: 2026-10-30 | Stage 2 · Pilot / feasibility | Readiness 44/100 | Voice: Piper en_US-lessac-medium (local)

HOOK
One in three patients labelled unresponsive could actually control a sound with their thoughts.

WHAT IT IS
It is a bedside EEG brain-wave cap that detects hidden awareness.

ANALOGY
Think of a ship's radio operator in a storm, picking out a faint SOS from a sailor who cannot shout, only tap.

HOW IT WORKS
First, patients try to open and close a hand. Second, the EEG reads that intent and changes the speed of a tone.

BEFORE
Before, these patients could look fully unresponsive.

AFTER
Now 13 of 41, 32 percent, showed control, and 46 percent of them regained independence at 12 months, versus 7 percent without.

WHY IT MATTERS
Early detection could change care. It is single-centre and small. EEG device makers and families gain. Our judgment: watch. Not financial advice.
