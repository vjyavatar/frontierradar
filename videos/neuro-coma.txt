FrontierRadar explainer: A bedside brain-wave cap let 1 in 3 'unresponsive' patients control a sound
Issue: 2026-10-30 | Stage 2 · Pilot / feasibility | Readiness 44/100 | Voice: Piper en_US-lessac-medium (local)

HOOK
Some very sick patients can't move, but their brains still listen.

WHAT IT IS
A bedside brain-wave cap let 1 in 3 'unresponsive' patients control a sound.

WHAT IT DOES
13 of 41 clinically unresponsive brain-injury patients (32%) controlled the speed of a tone by attempting to open and close a hand, read by EEG. Think of it like this: Like finding someone locked in a dark room by giving them a light switch, and seeing them flick it.

BEFORE
Some patients are conscious but cannot move or speak.

AFTER
Who could benefit: EEG and neuro-monitoring device makers and Hospitals and families.

WHY IT MATTERS
We rate it Stage 2, Pilot / feasibility, readiness 44 out of 100. The catch: Single centre, 41 patients. Our judgment: watch. Not financial advice.
