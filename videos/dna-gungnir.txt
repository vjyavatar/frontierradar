FrontierRadar explainer: HKU's 'Gungnir' code recovers data from DNA damaged like 400-year-old archives
Issue: 2026-10-30 | Stage 1 · Lab proof | Readiness 28/100 | Voice: Piper en_US-lessac-medium (local)

HOOK
DNA damaged like a 400-year-old archive just gave back every bit of its data.

WHAT IT IS
Gungnir is a new error-repair code for data stored in DNA, from the University of Hong Kong.

ANALOGY
Think of a locksmith facing a rusted safe. He tries combination after combination, and a click tells him the moment one is right.

HOW IT WORKS
First, the computer guesses what each damaged piece originally said. Second, it checks every guess against a stored fingerprint, an idea borrowed from blockchains, and keeps only the match.

BEFORE
Before, codes gave up when too many letters were wrong.

AFTER
Now it tolerates error rates up to 20 percent, which HKU calls a fourfold improvement, and recovered data losslessly.

WHY IT MATTERS
More computing buys stronger repair. DNA tool makers and archive providers could gain, but writing DNA is still slow and costly. Our judgment: wait. Not financial advice.
