Issue: 2026-10-31 | Stage 2 · Early trial (Ph 1/2) | Readiness 57/100 | Voice: Piper en_US-lessac-medium (local)

HOOK
America's drug regulator may let one approval cover many custom gene edits.

WHAT IT IS
It is FDA draft guidance for personalised gene-editing treatments for rare diseases.

ANALOGY
Think of a tailor approved once for a suit pattern. After that, each customer's size can be cut without a fresh approval, as long as the measurements stay in the approved range.

HOW IT WORKS
First, a company defines the limits within which its therapy may vary. Second, new patient versions inside those limits are added with lab data, without new clinical trials.

BEFORE
Before, an edit for one family's rare mutation looked like a brand-new drug needing its own trials.

AFTER
Now, if finalised, many custom versions could share one approval. Comments close on November 27.

WHY IT MATTERS
This could open rare-disease markets that were too small to serve. It is still a draft. Our judgment: watch. Not financial advice.
