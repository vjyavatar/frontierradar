FrontierRadar explainer: China's DF1000 AI chip stacks memory directly on its logic, reaching 6.4 TB/s without HBM
Issue: 2026-10-31 | Stage 2 · Prototype | Readiness 52/100 | Voice: Piper en_US-lessac-medium (local)

HOOK
A Chinese AI chip skips the world's scarcest memory and still claims 6.4 terabytes per second.

WHAT IT IS
DF1000, from Dongfang Suanxin, stacks a memory wafer directly on top of the chip's logic.

ANALOGY
Think of building the warehouse directly above the shop floor, linked by thousands of lifts, instead of trucking goods in from a depot across town.

HOW IT WORKS
First, a memory wafer is bonded face to face onto the logic wafer, with no bumps in between. Second, the very short, dense links feed data fast, even on an older 14 nanometre process.

BEFORE
Before, AI chips sat beside H B M memory stacks that are scarce and restricted for China.

AFTER
Now a 128-card cluster ran 72 hours non-stop, with a 1,000-card cluster planned by year end.

WHY IT MATTERS
If the claims hold, memory-on-logic stacking could reshape AI chips. Figures are the company's own. Our judgment: watch. Not financial advice.
